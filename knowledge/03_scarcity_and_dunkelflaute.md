# Price spikes, scarcity and "Dunkelflaute"

## Dunkelflaute
"Dunkelflaute" (dark doldrums) describes periods with very little wind and solar output, typically in winter under a stable high-pressure system. Residual load then approaches total demand, and gas plants, peakers and imports are needed. Prices in the evening peak (17:00-20:00) can rise far above normal levels.

## Historical reference events (DE-LU day-ahead, hourly prices before October 2025)
- 6 November 2024: a prolonged Dunkelflaute. Evening hourly prices exceeded 800 EUR/MWh.
- 12 December 2024: low wind and cold weather. The hourly price reached about 936 EUR/MWh in the evening, among the highest since the 2022 energy crisis.
- Both events attracted public debate about market power and about whether enough dispatchable capacity was available.

## Typical signature in the data
- Wind generation well below its seasonal average (often below 5 GW combined onshore and offshore).
- High residual load, especially in the evening.
- Large imports (negative net position) as Germany draws on neighbouring markets.
- High output from gas and hard coal plants.
- A sharp evening spike, sometimes also a morning peak around 08:00.

## Summer spikes
Evening price spikes can also happen in summer. Solar output drops away quickly in the evening while demand (including cooling) stays high. If wind is weak at the same time, the evening ramp is steep. This is the "neck" of the duck curve.

## Interpreting spikes
When the price spikes, check: (1) residual load in the spike hours, (2) the generation mix in those hours (which technology was marginal), (3) imports and exports, and (4) whether the wind or solar forecast was too optimistic. A forecast miss mostly affects intraday and imbalance prices rather than the day-ahead price.
