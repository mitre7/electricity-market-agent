# German power market: key facts

## Structure
- Bidding zone: DE-LU (Germany and Luxembourg), a single price zone.
- Four transmission system operators (TSOs): 50Hertz, Amprion, TenneT DE, TransnetBW.
- Exchanges: EPEX SPOT and Nord Pool (day-ahead and intraday); EEX (futures).
- Germany is interconnected with France, the Netherlands, Belgium, Denmark, Poland, Czechia, Austria, Switzerland, Sweden and Norway. Since 2023 Germany has more often been a net importer than a net exporter in the day-ahead market, especially in summer evenings and in periods with low wind.

## Installed capacity (approximate, end of 2025)
- Solar PV: about 115 GW, growing by about 15 GW per year.
- Onshore wind: about 66 GW. Offshore wind: about 9.7 GW.
- Lignite: about 15 GW. Hard coal: about 15 GW, with part held in reserve. Natural gas: about 35 GW.
- Nuclear: 0 GW since 15 April 2023.
- Battery storage: about 20 GWh, mostly home storage, with large-scale storage growing quickly.
These figures are rounded and approximate. Use them only for orders of magnitude.

## Typical daily demand
Load is typically about 55-70 GW on winter weekdays, about 45-60 GW on summer weekdays, and about 10-15 GW lower on Sundays and public holidays. The grid load published by ENTSO-E excludes most self-consumption from rooftop PV.

## Public holidays (nationwide)
2025: 1 Jan, 18 Apr (Good Friday), 21 Apr (Easter Monday), 1 May (Labour Day), 29 May (Ascension), 9 Jun (Whit Monday), 3 Oct (German Unity Day), 25-26 Dec.
2026: 1 Jan, 3 Apr (Good Friday), 6 Apr (Easter Monday), 1 May (Labour Day), 14 May (Ascension), 25 May (Whit Monday), 3 Oct (German Unity Day), 25-26 Dec.
Holidays behave like Sundays: lower demand, and more negative prices in spring and summer.
